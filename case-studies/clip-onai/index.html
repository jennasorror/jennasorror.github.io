Clip-onAI

Making AI analytics easier to understand at a glance.

As the product designer, I helped an early-stage computer-vision startup turn activity captured through handheld tools into understandable analytics. The initial use case involved a litter grabber and a volunteer cleanup organization.

My role

Product Designer · UX Researcher

Duration

6 months

Team

1 product designer · 2 developers · 1 business analyst · 1 project manager

10

participants

Participants tested dashboard prototypes every two weeks over three months.

In this storyUnderstanding the userEarly explorationUseful defaultsClearer chartsAI summariesOutcome & lesson
01Understanding the user

Start with the person interpreting the data.

The project persona captured the need for understandable metrics, quick environmental-impact insights, and simple visuals. Those needs informed the default widgets and clearer chart explanations that follow.

02Early exploration

More data did not mean more understanding.

The initial litter-grabber use case brought together users with varied technical familiarity, but the dashboard felt dense and overly technical. People struggled with unfamiliar metrics, too many visualization options, and identifying useful insights. I used low-fidelity prototypes in biweekly sessions to understand where interpretation broke down.

03Useful defaults

Useful defaults, with room to customize.

The first barrier was choosing what to display. Users had difficulty filtering through widget types, so I designed fixed default widgets as a ready-made starting point. Customizable layouts offered flexibility after that initial structure, letting users arrange information without first having to build a dashboard from scratch.

04Clearer charts

Explain what the chart is showing.

A clearer layout still needed clearer meaning inside each widget. Participants asked for more explanation of the analytics, so I refined chart labels and legends to identify the variables being shown. This made interpretation, rather than the number of visualization options, the focus of the design.

05AI summaries

Add a plain-language route into the data.

Even with better chart context, users wanted a faster way to understand the data. The Insights widget added an AI-generated summary alongside the visualizations. It offered another route into the information, although summary accuracy and gains in comprehension were not established by the available testing evidence.

06Outcome & lesson

Design for interpretation before adding options.

These findings shaped the progression from low-fidelity prototypes to the final dashboard: useful defaults, clearer labels, then flexible layouts and summaries. Participant feedback supported the value of the visuals, but a standardized satisfaction measure was not documented. I learned to prioritize interpretation before adding options; future testing should measure comprehension and trust in AI summaries directly.
