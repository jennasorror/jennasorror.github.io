Clip-onAI

Making AI analytics easier to understand at a glance.

At Clip-onAI, I designed dashboards for data captured during litter collection. I tested the high-fidelity screens with 10 participants to understand how they read the heat maps, category breakdowns, and session counts. Their feedback also led me to design an AI-generated Insights widget.

01Product & people

The cleanup context

Clip-onAI started with a practical idea: attach camera sensors to existing tools and capture information as people work. Litter surveying was an early pilot. A grabber could help collect useful data as well as litter, giving communities a clearer picture of their roadsides.

The idea grew out of the founder’s roadside volunteering and photographs of collected litter. My work focused on the dashboard: how could I help someone understand the collected data without needing to know every metric first?

Who would use the dashboard?

The persona helped me think about what someone would need when opening the dashboard. I used it to work through which information to show first and where the charts would need more explanation.

02Information flow

From computer vision to human decisions

The camera concept meant volunteers could keep collecting rather than stop to tally each item. Computer vision would help interpret the images. I worked on the handoff from those outputs to something a person could read: choosing labels, organizing information, and giving the charts enough context to make sense.

03Testing the dashboard

What participants needed from the dashboard

I tested the high-fidelity dashboard screens with 10 participants in biweekly sessions over three months. I wanted to understand how they chose which information to look at and how they interpreted the charts. The screens below show the dashboard layout and visualizations we tested. The request for simpler summaries led to the Insights widget.

04Useful defaults

A ready-to-use dashboard layout

The high-fidelity dashboard opens with a set of default widgets, so someone can start reading the data without first choosing every visualization. The layout is also customizable for people who want to focus on different information.

05Clearer charts

Reading categories and locations

In these screens, each chart has a title and labels that identify what is being measured. The category charts support comparisons between litter types, while the heat map shows collection activity by location. I paid attention to those details during testing to understand where people needed more explanation.

1 of 3

  1. The bar chart compares how often each litter category appears in the collected data.
  2. The donut chart shows each category’s share of collected litter. The table adds counts and changes for comparison.
  3. The heat map shows where litter collection activity is concentrated. Its color scale helps readers compare the relative frequency across locations.
06AI summaries

Summaries alongside the charts

In testing, participants asked for simpler summaries alongside the charts. That feedback led me to design the Insights widget.

USABILITY TESTING / RESEARCH SUMMARY

From research to the Insights widget

Finding

Participants appreciated customizable data displays but requested simpler summaries for quicker understanding.

Design response

I added the Insights widget to summarize the available data alongside the charts.

“I appreciate the different types of visuals like the heat map, but it's sometimes hard for me to quickly understand what the data is telling me. I think a simpler summary would be helpful”

Usability testing · Participant 3 · Volunteer, age 45

We checked the summaries for accuracy and tested comprehension. The summaries were accurate, and participants reported that they understood the information better.

07Retrospective exploration

Retrospective design concept · not shipped

When the summary is wrong

The summaries were accurate in our testing. When I revisited the widget, I wanted to explore what someone could do if an insight looked wrong. These screens show a way to check the supporting data and flag a specific sentence.

1 of 3

What I’d test next: Can people verify a claim, flag an issue, and understand the confirmation? These concepts use example data and have not been tested.

08Outcome & lesson

What I learned

The high-fidelity screens gave me something concrete to discuss with the 10 participants during biweekly testing over three months. Their feedback helped me understand what they needed from the dashboard and informed the Insights widget. We checked the summaries for accuracy, and participants reported that they understood the information better.

Testing helped me understand why someone might value detailed charts and still want a short explanation alongside them. I also worked with engineers on reusable interface patterns and a visual style guide to keep the dashboard consistent.

Revisiting the widget made me think more about what happens when someone disagrees with an insight. In the future, I’d like to test whether the supporting data gives them enough context to check it, and whether flagging an issue feels straightforward.
