dear. · UX case study

Making room for connection, without making reflection a requirement.

I co-designed a collaborative journaling app concept for first-year college students navigating loneliness, distance, and personal change. The experience balances private reflection with lightweight connection to friends.

01Student needs

Reflection alone did not address disconnection.

Surveys with 20+ first-year students surfaced loneliness, changing routines, and distance from familiar support systems. Participants valued shared experiences, photos, music, and milestones. Alongside benchmarking of private journaling tools, these findings shifted our direction toward a journal that could also support connection.

A separate barriers question received six responses: five mentioned uncertainty about what to write, and four each cited lack of time and difficulty staying consistent. This reinforced the need for prompts and flexible participation; this question has fewer responses than the 21-response sharing-preferences chart.

02Testing the assumption

A daily habit could become another obligation.

We translated those needs into low-fidelity wireframes and digitized them for testing. Our early flow used daily check-in and check-out prompts to encourage a habit. Contextual inquiry and cognitive walkthroughs challenged that assumption: students wanted to skip reflection on difficult days without feeling that they had failed to participate.

03Revised flow

Give students permission to participate on their terms.

That feedback changed the flow. We made participation optional and introduced more flexible entry points, allowing students to decide when to reflect and when to connect. Group features and multimedia entries retained the shared experiences students valued, while removing the expectation of a daily routine.

  1. Choose whether to reflect
  2. Write privately or connect through a group
  3. Add meaningful photos, music, or milestones
  4. Return when ready

Reading guide to the revised experience; the original detailed flow is preserved below.

04Visual system

A consistent visual language for reflection and connection.

The style guide brings the project’s typography, color palette, and interface elements into one reference. These shared foundations carry through the final journaling and group experiences.

05Final experience

Keep connection lightweight and personal.

The final screens brought these decisions together: reflection prompts alongside groups, photos, music, and milestones, with flexibility about when to participate. My contributions spanned research, UX writing, interaction flows, and prototypes within the team. The experience aimed to support connection without turning reflection into another obligation.

06Outcome & lesson

Interest was promising; sustained use remains untested.

In testing, 19 of 20 participants said they would use the app regularly. That signals interest in the concept, rather than observed engagement or retention. The project’s central lesson was to let students control the rhythm of reflection. Future work should examine voluntary use over time and whether group participation supports connection without adding pressure.
