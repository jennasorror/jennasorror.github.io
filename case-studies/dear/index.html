dear.

Making room for connection, without making reflection a requirement.

Starting college can mean building a new routine while missing the people who know you best. I co-designed dear., a journaling app concept that gives first-year students space to reflect privately and share small moments with friends.

01Student needs

What students wanted to share

We surveyed 20+ first-year students about the changes they were navigating. Loneliness and distance from familiar support came up alongside a desire to share photos, music, and milestones. Comparing those responses with private journaling tools helped us see an opportunity for reflection and connection in the same experience.

We also asked students what made journaling difficult. Five mentioned not knowing what to write; four each mentioned finding time and staying consistent. Those responses led me toward gentle prompts and a flow that could fit around an irregular routine.

02Testing the assumption

Testing daily check-ins

Our first wireframes included daily check-in and check-out prompts. We thought a routine might help, but contextual inquiry and cognitive walkthroughs showed us that students wanted to skip reflection on difficult days. I hadn’t considered how easily a daily prompt could start to feel like another obligation.

I took that feedback into the next flow: students should decide when reflection feels useful.

03Revised flow

Making participation optional

We made participation optional and added flexible entry points for reflection and connection. I kept the group and multimedia features students valued, while removing the expectation that they had to follow a daily routine.

  1. Choose whether to reflect
  2. Write privately or connect through a group
  3. Add meaningful photos, music, or milestones
  4. Return when ready

Optional participation provides entry points for private reflection or shared experiences, without requiring a daily routine.

Low-fidelity check-in and check-out wireframes show the initial habit-building assumption that students challenged.

The high-fidelity screens offer “Later” for both prompts. After dismissing a prompt, students can return through the check-in or check-out link on Home.

04Visual system

Colors and interface patterns

I used the same palette and interface patterns for private entries and group sharing. I wanted moving between them to feel familiar, even if someone mainly used one part of the app.

05Final experience

High-fidelity screens

The final screens bring optional prompts together with groups, photos, music, and milestones. Within the team, I contributed research, UX writing, interaction flows, and prototypes. I wanted students to recognize a small way to participate, whether that meant writing privately or sharing something with a friend.

06Outcome & lesson

Testing response

In testing, 19 of 20 participants said they would use dear. regularly. Their feedback helped shape an experience where students could reflect when they felt ready and stay connected through shared moments. Giving them that choice became an important part of the design.
