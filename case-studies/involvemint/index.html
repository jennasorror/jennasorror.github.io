involveMINT

Helping volunteers join projects and submit their impact with confidence.

At involveMINT, I worked on the steps volunteers followed to join a sustainability project and submit their Proof of Impact. Research showed that people were unsure how to get started and when their submission was complete. I redesigned the flow with those points of confusion in mind.

01Volunteer needs

Understanding where volunteers got stuck

In interviews and contextual inquiry with 15 volunteers, I heard uncertainty at both ends of the journey: how do I begin, and am I actually done? I mapped the flow and redesigned the interface with another UX designer and a project manager, focusing on unclear steps, backtracking, and missing submission feedback.

“I would finish one step but wasn’t sure if I was done or if I needed to do something else.”

Participant #7 · Testing the original workflow
02Project entry

A more direct route to the project

I tackled project entry first. Finding the right project took more navigation than it needed to, so I introduced QR-code entry. A volunteer could reach the relevant project from where they were volunteering, without searching through a list of other options.

03Progress & feedback

Showing progress and confirming submission

After project entry, I focused on helping volunteers follow the submission steps. I adjusted the hierarchy and added progress indicators. I also included a confirmation so people would know their submission was complete, which had been a point of confusion in research.

Progress indicators and submission confirmation make the sequence and its endpoint explicit.

The annotated original screens record the unclear hierarchy and completion feedback that informed the redesign.

04Testing

Results from usability sessions

I compared the testing records to see where project entry, progression, and feedback had improved. Reported completion rose from 60% to about 95% across the recorded tasks.

05Outcome & lesson

What I learned

All 15 participants described the redesigned experience as more intuitive. Seeing that feedback alongside the task results helped me understand which changes were useful. I came away paying more attention to the end of a flow: people need to know their work has gone through, just as much as they need to know how to begin.
