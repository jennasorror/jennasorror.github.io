Locus Robotics · UX case study

From 5–7 steps to one: making target management self-service.

I led the end-to-end UX design of Site Targets within LocusHub, helping warehouse teams compare performance with expected goals and enabling external clients to set and manage their own targets.

My role

UI/UX Designer · UX Researcher

Duration

3 months

Team

2 UX designers · 3 engineering developers · 1 project lead

✦Workflow change
5–7 → 1
≈150 intended users14 stakeholders reviewed designs

Workflow steps to set targets

Target-setting moved from a multi-team workflow to client self-service.

Step-count comparison · Equivalent to 80–86% fewer steps; time savings were not measured.

01The challenge

Performance data needed a point of reference.

Users could see warehouse metrics, but had no built-in reference for expected performance. Setting targets happened outside LocusHub and required internal and external handoffs. I framed the work around two connected needs: making performance understandable and giving clients control over their targets.

02Reading status

Fewer deviation states made the signal clearer.

I started with how users would recognize a deviation. Early research and internal testing showed that too many states created uncertainty, so I narrowed deviations to Moderate and Severe. Pairing symbols with color helped users distinguish status without relying on color alone.

03Chart decisions

Match target indicators to how each chart is read.

With the status system defined, I explored how it should appear across chart types. Target overlays gave bar and line charts a useful reference. In combination charts, I avoided bar coloration that could obscure the secondary line’s validation state; tables used row-level indicators to keep values scannable.

04Engineering tradeoff

Keep the signal within the platform’s limits.

I then reviewed these visualization concepts with developers. The charts could not support custom icons paired with text, so I proposed text-compatible emoji indicators. This preserved the status signal within the existing platform, with less control over how symbols would render across environments.

05Client self-service

Give clients control at the point of need.

Making targets visible addressed only half the problem: clients still needed to manage them. I designed a centralized settings page where external clients could define, edit, and save targets for individual metrics. This brought target-setting into LocusHub and removed the internal handoffs required for each update.

06Stakeholder review

Make the workflow concrete across roles.

To communicate the complete experience, I created and demoed four role-based scenarios and reviewed designs with 14 internal and external stakeholders. One followed an Operations Manager updating targets for incoming volume, then checking performance against those expectations. These reviews helped stakeholders assess the proposed experience across different roles and provided feedback for refinement.

07Outcome & lesson

One step changed who could do the work.

Together, the visualization and settings work connected performance monitoring with client-controlled targets. The documented target-setting workflow moved from 5–7 steps to one, equivalent to 80–86% fewer steps. This is a workflow comparison; time savings and downstream business gains were not quantified. The key lesson was to design the interpretation of data and the actions around it as one experience.
