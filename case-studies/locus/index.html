Locus Robotics

From 5–7 steps to one: making target management self-service.

I led the end-to-end UX design of Site Targets within LocusHub, helping warehouse teams compare performance with expected goals and enabling external clients to set and manage their own targets.

01The challenge

A reference for warehouse performance

Warehouse teams could see their metrics, but they couldn’t compare them with expected targets inside LocusHub. Setting those targets also meant coordinating with internal teams. I worked on bringing both tasks into LocusHub so clients could compare performance and manage their own targets.

02Reading status

Simplifying deviation states

I started with what a client needed to notice when a metric moved off target. Early research and internal testing showed that too many states made that harder, so I narrowed deviations to Moderate and Severe. I paired symbols with color so status wouldn’t depend on color alone.

03Chart decisions

Choosing indicators for each chart

Next, I explored how those states should work across the existing charts. Bar and line charts benefited from target overlays. Combination charts needed more care: coloring the bars could obscure the secondary line’s status. I adjusted the treatment for each chart and used row-level indicators in tables.

How the status treatment changes with the chart
ViewDecisionReason
Bar & areaKeep the target reference visible alongside deviation indicators.The comparison needs an explicit baseline.
CombinationAvoid a bar treatment that hides the secondary line’s state.Two data series must remain distinguishable.
04Engineering tradeoff

Working with chart constraints

When I reviewed the concepts with developers, I learned that the charts could not support custom icons paired with text. I proposed text-compatible emoji indicators as an alternative. They fit the chart implementation, though we had less control over how the symbols would render.

05Client self-service

Bringing target settings into LocusHub

Clients could now see their targets, but they still needed a way to change them. I designed a centralized settings page where they could define, edit, and save targets for individual metrics themselves. That connected the charts to a practical action and removed the internal handoffs for each update.

  1. ReviewSee current targets and performance.
  2. EditOpen Site Info → Targets and change metric values.
  3. SaveReturn to read-only values, then review the updated target reference.
06Stakeholder review

Reviewing four role-based scenarios

I created four role-based scenarios to walk stakeholders through the full experience. In one, an Operations Manager updated incoming-volume targets and checked performance against them. Reviewing these scenarios with 14 internal and external stakeholders gave us a concrete way to discuss the flow and refine the designs together.

07Outcome & lesson

Adoption and workflow results

Site Targets was adopted by approximately 150 users. Setting a target went from 5–7 steps involving internal teams to one client-controlled step, an 80–85.7% reduction. I learned a lot from working through the chart constraints with engineers and keeping the settings flow connected to the performance information clients were already using.
